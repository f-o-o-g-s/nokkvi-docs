The now-playing **metadata strip** — title, artist, album, and the track's format — can sit in a few different places, set by [`track_info_display`](/reference/config/#interface-settings). Default is `player_bar_above`, a strip on top of the bottom player bar's controls; the others move the strip under the controls or into the top chrome, fold it into a mini player with the cover, or hide it to give the list and artwork the whole window.

<Tabs syncKey="track-info-display">
  <TabItem label="Player Bar Above">
    The metadata sits as a strip in the bottom player bar, on top of the transport row, between the list and the controls. Beside the artwork column it's as wide as the player bar: the list's width.

    ![Metadata strip above the transport row in the bottom player bar](../../../assets/track-info-player-bar-above.webp)
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Mini Player">
    Artwork, metadata, and transport merge into one bar topped by a full-width progress scrub that shows elapsed and total time alongside the track's format and bitrate. On a wide window it spreads into three sections — metadata on the left, transport centered, modes and volume on the right. As the window narrows it folds into a single compact cluster with the mode toggles tucked behind a kebab (⋮) menu.

    ![Mini player on a wide window: artwork and metadata left, centered transport, modes and volume right, full-width progress scrub on top](../../../assets/track-info-mini-player.webp)

    ![Mini player on a narrow window: a single compact cluster with the mode toggles folded into the kebab menu](../../../assets/track-info-mini-player-compact.webp)

    The scrub has its own [seek bar style](/guides/library-basics/#seek-bar-styles) ([`mini_player_seekbar_style`](/reference/config/#interface-settings)), separate from the main player bar's. The default, Capsule, puts the times, format and bitrate inside the bar; every other style puts them beside the track.

    Hide the cover, the volume slider or the mode toggles independently under **Settings → Interface → Player Bar → Visible Controls** (shown while the mini player is the active layout), or with [`mini_player_show_artwork`](/reference/config/#interface-settings), [`mini_player_show_volume`](/reference/config/#interface-settings) and [`mini_player_show_modes`](/reference/config/#interface-settings).

    The mini player suits a layout without the [artwork panel](/guides/artwork/#the-artwork-panel) (`artwork_column_mode = "never"`): its thumbnail keeps the playing album's cover in sight on every view. With the artwork panel and the list thumbnails already showing the cover, turn the thumbnail off: the title, artist and album then start at the left edge, lined up with the elapsed time above them.

    ![Mini player with the cover hidden: title, artist and album at the left edge, under the elapsed time](../../../assets/track-info-mini-player-no-artwork.webp)
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Player Bar">
    The metadata sits as a strip in the bottom player bar, just below the transport row.

    ![Metadata strip below the transport row in the bottom player bar](../../../assets/track-info-player-bar.webp)
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Top Bar">
    The metadata rides inline in the top navigation row, to the right of the view tabs. In side-nav or no-nav layouts it sits as its own row above the slot list instead.

    ![Metadata merged into the top navigation row, to the right of the view tabs](../../../assets/track-info-top-bar.webp)
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Top Bar Under">
    The metadata gets its own row directly beneath the top navigation bar, as wide as the nav bar: beside the artwork column, that's the list's width.

    ![Metadata as its own row beneath the top navigation bar](../../../assets/track-info-top-bar-under.webp)
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Off">
    No metadata strip — the list and artwork get the full window.

    ![Nokkvi with no metadata strip, giving the list and artwork the full window](../../../assets/track-info-off.webp)
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

Which fields the strip shows, and whether they carry `title:` / `artist:` / `album:` labels, is controlled by the `strip_show_*` keys in the [configuration reference](/reference/config/#interface-settings).

## Bit-Perfect Status Badge

When [bit-perfect output](/guides/audio/#bit-perfect-output) is engaged, the now-playing strip carries a small badge reporting the *real* state of your sound card's clock — read from `/proc/asound`, not the rate nokkvi requested. It shows only while bit-perfect output is in Strict or Relaxed mode and a local track is playing; radio is excluded, since a network stream can't be bit-perfect.

![The mini-player progress scrub showing the BIT-PERFECT badge beside the track's FLAC 44.1 kHz format and bitrate](../../../assets/track-info-bit-perfect-badge.webp)

| Badge | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `BIT-PERFECT` | The device is clocked at the track's sample rate. Audio is reaching the DAC untouched — no resampling, no DSP. |
| `RESAMPLED→96k` | The device is locked at a different rate (shown in kHz — e.g. `→96k`), so PipeWire is resampling. When it can, the badge appends the app holding the device at that rate (`RESAMPLED→96k · Zen`), so you know what to close. |
| `UNVERIFIED` | Nokkvi can't read the device clock. Usually a Bluetooth sink (which re-encodes the audio, so bit-perfect isn't possible) or an idle / suspended device. |

A brief blank moment right as a track starts is normal — nokkvi waits for the device to settle before committing to a state, so it won't flash a false `UNVERIFIED`. In **Relaxed** mode the badge also drops for the duration of a same-rate crossfade — the overlapping blend isn't bit-perfect — and returns once the next track settles.
